**STYLE PROFILE**

1. **SENTENCE RHYTHM**
   - **Average Length**: The sentences are generally concise, averaging around 15-20 words.
   - **Range**: Sentences range from short, punchy statements (5-10 words) to more detailed explanations (25-30 words).
   - **Variance Distribution**: The author frequently alternates between short and medium-length sentences to maintain a dynamic flow.
   - **Typical Opening Constructions**: Sentences often begin with direct questions, factual statements, or key player highlights.

2. **LEXICAL SIGNATURE**
   - **Repeated Words/Phrases**: "average", "strike rate", "wickets", "runs", "batting", "bowling", "powerplay", "match-winning", "home advantage", "impact player", "chasing", "total", "economy", "form", "performance".
   - **Conspicuously Avoided Words**: "luck", "random", "fluke", "unpredictable", "guess", "maybe", "perhaps", "uncertain", "vague", "unplanned".

3. **REGISTER**
   - **Formality Level**: Semi-formal, with a focus on factual information and analysis.
   - **Addressing the Reader**: The reader is addressed indirectly through predictions and analyses rather than direct engagement.
   - **Opinion**: The author allows for opinion primarily through predictions and betting tips, maintaining a tone of informed speculation.
   - **Humour**: Minimal use of humour; the tone remains serious and analytical.

4. **PARAGRAPH ARCHITECTURE**
   - **Typical Length**: Paragraphs are generally medium-length, around 4-6 sentences.
   - **Openings**: Often open with a key fact or a question to set the stage for analysis.
   - **Closures**: Close with a prediction or a summary of the analysis.
   - **Transitions**: Smooth transitions using logical connectors or by introducing the next key point or player.

5. **DEVICES**
   - **Recurring Rhetorical Moves**: 
     - **Rhetorical Questions**: Frequently used to engage the reader and introduce predictions.
     - **Asides**: Occasional use to provide additional context or interesting stats.
     - **Callbacks**: References to previous matches or player performances.
     - **Understatement**: Rarely used; the tone is more direct and assertive.
     - **Direct Challenges**: Not used; the focus is on analysis rather than challenging the reader.

6. **TEN RULES**
   1. Open each article with a direct question related to the match outcome.
   2. Use bullet points or numbered lists for key statistics and predictions.
   3. Include at least one rhetorical question per article to engage the reader.
   4. Mention specific players' recent performances with exact statistics.
   5. Avoid using words that imply uncertainty, such as "maybe" or "perhaps".
   6. Use the phrase "match-winning" to describe key performances or players.
   7. Begin one in every three paragraphs with a factual statement or statistic.
   8. Conclude each team preview with a prediction or betting tip.
   9. Use the word "impact player" to highlight key contributors in the match.
   10. Maintain a semi-formal tone, focusing on analysis and predictions rather than personal opinions or humour.
